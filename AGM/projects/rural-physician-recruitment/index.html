Beyond Financial Incentives: Lessons on Rural Physician Recruitment

Examining the effectiveness of financial incentives for physician supply.

RESEARCH STATUSArticle presented as published in Social Science & Medicine (2026)FOCUSHealth policy · Rural health · Labour economics
Explore the original poster ↗← Back to projects
View full poster ↗
01 / THE BIG QUESTION

What is this research about?

In Canada, rural and remote communities account for about 18% of the population and are served by about 8% of physicians. This research asks whether Saskatchewan's Rural Physician Incentive Program changed the rural-urban distribution of family physicians.

“Can direct financial payments narrow the rural–urban physician supply gap?”
02 / THE APPROACH

How the work comes together.

  • Use Saskatchewan's Rural Physician Incentive Program as a natural experiment.
  • Combine Canadian Institute for Health Information physician data and Statistics Canada population data.
  • Analyze the period from 2009 to 2021.
03 / WHAT WE'RE LEARNING

Findings & progress.

  • Rural physician rates remained persistently below urban rates.
  • The study found no statistically robust evidence that the incentive program narrowed the rural-urban physician supply gap.
  • The authors suggest financial payments alone may be insufficient where structural barriers remain, including professional isolation, family employment, infrastructure and rural practice support.
  • The charts compare physician rates in mixed Saskatchewan regions and the rural-urban gap in Saskatchewan and Manitoba, with 2013 marked as the policy introduction. The written findings do not claim that the program closed the gap.

WHAT THIS COULD MEAN

The findings inform policy conversations about physician recruitment and rural service access, without demonstrating that a specific alternative intervention would succeed.
