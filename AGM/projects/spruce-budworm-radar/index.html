Spruce Budworm Radar Segmentation

Can weather radar help us detect insect migrations before outbreaks spread?

RESEARCH STATUSExperimental researchFOCUSArtificial intelligence · Forest health · Remote sensing

View full poster ↗
01 / THE BIG QUESTION

What is this research about?

Migrating spruce budworm moths can carry destructive forest outbreaks across large distances. This study investigates whether deep learning can identify moth swarms in weather radar and follow their movements through the night.

“Can weather radar and deep learning reliably identify migrating spruce budworm swarms?”
02 / THE APPROACH

How the work comes together.

  • Use weather radar volumes and expert annotations of budworm swarms.
  • Train an Attention U-Net to segment swarm patterns.
  • Compare predicted masks with annotations using Dice, precision and recall.
03 / WHAT WE'RE LEARNING

Findings & progress.

  • The poster reports analysis of 2,052 radar scenes across 57 experiments.
  • Overall experimental results: macro Dice 0.635, precision 0.636, and recall 0.827.
  • Examples show that prediction quality varies across the night and across radar scenes.

WHAT THIS COULD MEAN

The research explores a potential new way to study migrating pests and support forest-health monitoring. Field deployment or operational early-warning performance is not claimed.
