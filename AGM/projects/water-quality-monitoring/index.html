Water & Environment

Community-Based Water Quality Monitoring in the Upper Great Lakes

Building local knowledge and capacity from Lake Superior to Lake Huron.

01 / THE BIG QUESTION

What is this research about?

From the lower Lake Superior watershed to the North Shore of Lake Huron, communities are leading efforts to understand and protect their waterways. The project supports training, field monitoring, tools and local decision-making.

“How can communities build the skills, evidence and partnerships to protect their own waters?”
02 / THE APPROACH

How the work comes together.

  • Support communities to identify their monitoring sites and priorities.
  • Provide training, field supplies and specialized expertise.
  • Carry out chemistry, eDNA and insect monitoring, and build regional methods.
03 / WHAT WE'RE LEARNING

Findings & progress.

  • From 2021 to 2026, more than 200 volunteers monitored 46 sites and collected more than 45,000 data points over 465 visits.
  • More than 10,000 people were reached through more than 40 public events, and the poster reports more than 75,000 social media impressions.
  • Sixty-seven youth were mentored, trained or hired: 52 Indigenous youth interns and hires from 15 First Nations across Canada, and 15 post-secondary placements.
  • Eight communities benefited from pilot programming; four now independently manage expanded permanent programs.
  • Two public campaigns, on microplastics and road salts in the St. Marys River, are reported as completed.
  • Two community-driven projects on nutrients and E. coli, in the lower Lake Superior watershed and on Lake Huron's North Shore, are reported as started, with completion planned by March 2027.
