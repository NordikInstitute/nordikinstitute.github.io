Research with care

This project concerns Residential School Survivors, families and Indigenous community leadership. The poster contains sensitive history; content is shared here for exhibition review. Public use, imagery and descriptions require approval from the appropriate community partners. Protected database records are not made available here.

01 / THE BIG QUESTION

What is this research about?

The Mino Bimaadiziwin initiative supports Survivors and families from communities affected by the Shingwauk Indian Residential School. Its work emphasizes culturally centred services, community partnerships and care directed by Indigenous knowledge and priorities.

“How can Indigenous-led support networks respond to the continuing impacts of Residential Schools?”
02 / THE APPROACH

How the work comes together.

  • Build relationships with health authorities, Knowledge Keepers and Elders.
  • Coordinate training, learning events and culturally centred workshops.
  • Develop a data-management initiative intended to share approved documents and resources.
03 / WHAT WE'RE LEARNING

Findings & progress.

  • The poster documents 2025 trauma training, community participation and a Kairos Blanket Exercise.
  • It describes the planned public launch of a resource dashboard as part of a project expected to conclude in spring 2027.
✳
WHAT THIS COULD MEAN

The project centres survivor and family wellbeing. Participation in activities is documented, while long-term health outcomes are not quantified in this poster.
